# Review, 2026-09-19: SWOT and the plan to ship

Before this review, everything was brought up to date: dependencies (Vite 8, Vitest 5, TypeScript 7, Wrangler 4, pnpm 12), CI actions, a fresh pull of every data source, and a re-check of every listing against its own page. Then five independent reviewers read the code, the data and the docs. They looked at privacy and security, the query rules, the web app, the pipeline, the API and steward page, data quality, and whether the docs match what's built. Every weakness below was reproduced or traced in the code. Nothing here is a guess.

## Strengths

- **Zero-PII is enforced, not just promised.** The Worker never reads an IP or user-agent header; tests scan the source to keep it so. Request bodies are closed schemas. The dedupe hash can't link one person's reports across places or days. Search text, ZIP, location, saved places and language never leave the phone or reach a URL.
- **What a phone shows can't be forged.** Bundles are Ed25519-signed (strict RFC 8032 checking), every file is checked against its checksum, an older bundle is refused, and 911 and 988 are hardcoded.
- **Honest freshness.** Badges are computed on the phone from dated facts. Unknown hours never read as "open." Reports label a listing; they never hide it.
- **Built for a cheap phone with bad signal.** 57 KB of app code, ES2020 output, offline after one visit, and our own street map with no map company in the loop.
- **Real, checked data.** 178 listings: 118 researched on each organization's own site and matched by machine or by a person, plus 60 stations from DHD's own layer. Every open greenway segment has help within a 10-minute walk. Neighborhoods with no food listed nearby went from 160 to 93 of 205.
- **One set of rules, three places.** The web app, the pipeline and the iPhone library run the same 77 fixture cases, in CI.
- **A civic data layer with guardrails.** Neighborhood pages for all 205 neighborhoods follow the docs/13 honesty rules: no rankings, small counts hidden, home prices shown beside building permits. The directory exports as HSDS 3.2 open data.
- **Made to be handed over.** Open source (Apache-2.0), docs for running it, a dead-man switch, a publish workflow that turns every open-data change into a pull request, and supply-chain safeguards (pnpm 12, pinned runners, no cache in the job that signs).
- **Spanish** on every screen, with a place's own words left in English and marked.

## Weaknesses

Grouped by what they put at risk. **Bold** items must be fixed before anyone relies on the app.

### Could send someone the wrong way (safety)
1. **Before the first data load, Urgent help and the overdose steps can't be reached.** A first visit with no signal shows plain text with no number to tap. The fix doesn't need data: 911 and 988 are hardcoded.
2. **A same-day cancellation doesn't close a pantry that's already open.** Future days can't be cancelled at all. "Always open" listings ignore cancellations. A listing's own page never shows an alert that targets it.
3. **The hours importer can invent hours.** "Mon 7-9pm" becomes 7am–9pm. "Call 24 hours ahead" becomes "open 24 hours." No row has this today; it's one research line away.
4. **The source check can match by accident.** It joins every digit on a page before searching, so scattered numbers can "match" a phone. A row with no phone and no street number passes automatically. The emergency-number check doesn't even strip HTML. This weakens the core freshness claim.
5. **Bad schedule values get through validation.** A non-numeric interval freezes every phone that loads the bundle. A bad month-day shows "open" on wrong days. "2nd Tuesday" on a weekly rule becomes every Tuesday.
6. **"A safe place to sleep → I'm under 25" lists after-school programs.** 16 of 19 results are tutoring and sports. Covenant House, the only youth shelter, is filed as `youth`, not shelter.
7. **A phone number with an extension dials a wrong number.** `tel:31357921004217`.
8. **An error answer from a City layer can empty a published file.** Every Narcan site would vanish from the next build instead of being held back.
9. Badge dates use the UTC day, so a report after 8pm shows tomorrow's date.
10. Brightmoor's pantry shows "open" on days its own page says it's closed. HUDA shows "open" after walk-ins end.
10a. **A crisis (mental-health) listing can show as a dot and card on a greenway segment map.** The segment screen filters out domestic-violence rows only. That breaks "sensitive listings never get a dot."
10b. **Restoring an archived listing records it as "checked by phone."** The badge then says "We checked by phone…" when nobody called, which breaks "never say verified for something no person checked."

### Could leak or keep what it shouldn't (privacy)
11. **The service worker caches steward pages and photos** on a steward's device. The "delete this photo" button and the 30-day deletion never reach that copy. It can also save the admin page as the offline app.
12. **One photo can stop the 180-day report deletion for the whole database.** The monthly counts then double every night.
13. Condition reports keep the minute they were received. That undoes the "to the hour" rule, and the minute is when the person was standing there.
14. Phone and email masking misses some fields: proposal what/hours and suggested corrections. Proposals are kept forever.
15. Steward actions accept a cross-site form post. Whether that works depends on the Access cookie setting, which we don't control in code. The admin page can also be framed.
16. Photos would turn on for the public the moment the R2 bucket exists: there is no switch.
17. A release web build can quietly trust the dev key. The deployed app then could never load real data.
18. One forged "still open" tap clears a "reported closed" label. Someone can hold the circuit breaker on, which hides genuine closure labels.

### Could fail quietly (reliability)
19. Back from a listing to a need screen drops the person on Home. Forward acts like Back.
20. After a failed first load, coming back online doesn't retry for 15 minutes.
21. A report or proposal made while the queue is sending can be lost. A 429 "slow down" answer counts as sent.
22. The "list is old" and sunset banners show on Home only. docs/12 promises them on every list and listing.
23. A machine check can move the heartbeat, which is meant to move only when a person acts.
24. The steward page can settle reports the steward never saw. Its queue fills up with "still open" taps and hides closure reports.
25. The web tests mostly read the source text. The router, the queues and the service worker have no behavior tests.

### Not done yet
26. Nothing is deployed. No domain, no Cloudflare resources, no signing keys.
27. **detroitmi.gov now blocks scripted requests.** City events are frozen at the last good file. The shelter line's page can't be machine-checked, so from 2026-10-18 a release build needs a person's check. 33 listings can only be checked by eye.
28. The iPhone screens have never been compiled. They have no reports, saved places, map or neighborhood pages yet.
29. Gaps in the data:
    - 93 of 205 neighborhoods still have no food listed nearby.
    - None of the ~18 DHD programs the docs promise are listed.
    - Only 2 shower listings.
    - Two phone numbers sit in free text where no check can see them.
30. Page watchers and the nightly re-check are described in many docs but not built.
30a. The emergency-number gate skips every three-digit number, so 211 is never checked (911 and 988 are hardcoded on purpose). Council districts come from the neighborhoods layer, which may not follow the 2026 district lines.
31. Legal and policy work isn't started:
    - photo-upload duties;
    - review of the overdose steps;
    - a privacy policy page;
    - the "not an official City app" line;
    - store labels and an organization developer account.

## Opportunities

- **The open dataset.** HSDS 3.2 is the format 211 and the Community Information Exchange use. A clean, checked, openly licensed directory could flow into systems people already call.
- **The greenway judge.** Condition reports plus the neighborhood greenway lens are the de-identified impact monitoring they asked for.
- **Partners who already care:**
  - Passenger Recovery's "Compass" meeting finder (a courtesy note, and a possible data partner);
  - 313SafeBeds;
  - libraries and community health workers as stewards;
  - Gleaners and Forgotten Harvest, with written permission.
- **Reach without app stores.** The web app installs from a link or a QR code on a flyer. It can go live as soon as it's deployed.
- **Southwest Detroit in Spanish now, Arabic next.**
- **Neighborhood pages as a civic tool** for block clubs, council staff and reporters, with honesty rules most dashboards lack.
- **A relationship with the City.** Allow-listing the pipeline, or a real events feed, would fix weakness 27 for good.

## Threats

- **Depending on detroitmi.gov.** Its blocking already froze events and threatens the release gate. More City pages may follow.
- **Legal exposure.**
  - Anonymous photo uploads and any duty to report illegal images.
  - The overdose steps.
  - Keeping DV information accurate and safe.
- **Abuse.** Fake closure reports, spam proposals (there's no Turnstile yet), and cross-site posts if the Access cookie allows them.
- **Stale data does harm.** Sending someone to a closed pantry costs them a bus fare and a meal. Without stewards, the dead-man switch is the only backstop, and today its banner shows on Home only.
- **Brand confusion** with the City's "Detroit Compassion App." A trademark search is still advised before store submission.
- **App store review.** Apple wants an organization account for this kind of app (5.1.1(ix)), and honest data labels.
- **One person.** No named stewards, no funding. The design assumes no one maintains anything, but someone has to press "publish."

## The plan to ship (recommended)

Each phase ends with tests, a browser check and a commit. Nothing goes public until Phases 1 and 2 are done.

1. **Safety and privacy fixes (weaknesses 1–18).** Each fix gets a test that fails first, and fixture cases join the shared suite so the iPhone library follows. This includes the hours importer, the source matcher shared with the emergency check, schedule validation, cancellations, the offline first run, the service worker, photo retention, the hour rule, masking, CSRF and framing headers, a photos switch that defaults to off, and a release-build key guard.
2. **Reliability (19–25).** Router history, retry and queue safety, banners on every screen, the heartbeat rule, the steward page settling only what it shows, and real behavior tests for the router, queues and service worker.
3. **Data.**
   - Fix the youth route and the two schedule mismatches.
   - Move free-text phone numbers into checked fields; add shower listings.
   - Check the 33 blocked listings and the shelter line by hand.
   - Record the checks.
   - If you agree: the DHD programs, and more research where food is thinnest.
4. **Ready to deploy.**
   - Everything that doesn't cost money: a preflight script, a privacy page, the "not official" line, the About reset button, and a documented step-by-step deploy.
   - Then your go-ahead for the domain, Cloudflare and keys.
5. **iPhone parity** (needs a Mac to compile). Reports, saved places and the map, ported against the same fixtures.
