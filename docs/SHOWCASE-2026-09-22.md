## Slide 3 — Solution

Headline: **It stores nothing. Freshness is the product. The data outlives the app.**

Three columns, one icon each:

1. **Zero personal data.** No account, name, phone or analytics. Location sorts on the phone and is never sent.
   A city that adopts it never touches resident data, because there isn't any.
2. **Honest freshness.** Every listing shows dated facts and a plain badge. Unknown is never shown as open.
   One-tap anonymous reports; a person, not an algorithm, archives. The nightly job re-reads every listing's own page.
3. **Open data.** The directory is published as HSDS 3.2, CC BY 4.0 — 211 or the City can take it today.

Footer strip: works offline · 4 languages · WCAG 2.2 AA audited · 81 KB of JavaScript · web, iPhone, Android.

Say: "Repeatable: two to four taps to a phone number. Scalable: a new city is a config file and a signed
bundle, not a fork."

If Arabic, Spanish or Bengali comes up: machine-drafted, no native speaker has reviewed them yet; review is on
the roadmap, crisis screens first.

## Slide 5 — Impact to Detroit If Adopted

Headline: **Fewer wasted trips. No new data risk. About $110 a year.**

- **Residents:** food, a bed, a clinic, Narcan in two to four taps, offline, in four languages, no sign-up.
  Urgent help — 911, 988, overdose steps — is one tap from every screen.
- **Helpers:** community health workers, librarians, 211 operators and church volunteers get one current list,
  and their reports make it better.
- **The City and DHD:** an open dataset they can publish or pull, and nothing to secure — no resident data exists.
- **Safety by design:** domestic-violence listings carry no address anywhere; private screens leave no trace.
- **It fails safe:** if everyone walks away, it tells people to call 211 instead of sending them to a closed door.
- Second pillar, one line: 11 transport layers and bus directions to every listing.

Big numbers: **531** listings · **205** neighborhoods · **4** languages · **0** bytes of resident data.

Do not claim users, trips saved or outcomes. It went live yesterday. Say what adoption *would* do.

## Slide 6 — Market and Opportunity

Headline: **The software is a gift. The service is the business.**

- **Who pays:** not residents, ever. Cities, health departments, 211s, food banks and greenway nonprofits pay for
  hosting, data stewardship, support and features — a yearly service agreement. Grants fund the first steps.
- **Why it can last:** about $110 a year to run and about an hour a week to steward. It survives with no revenue.
- **Why it spreads:** a region is a signed bundle plus a config file. HSDS means it plugs into any 211. National
  sources (SAMHSA, US DOT, Census, GTFS) are already wired. Zero PII means no per-state privacy rework.
- **Path:** Detroit → a second Michigan city (Flint or Grand Rapids) → Michigan with Michigan 211 → hosted
  "region in a box" → national.
- **Never ads, never data.** There is nothing about residents to sell, by construction.
- **Ask: [Kyle: choose one]** an introduction to DHD or Michigan 211 · a pilot steward partner · funding for
  native-speaker review and store releases.

Close: "It costs about a hundred dollars a year, holds no resident data, and if everyone walks away it says
call 211. It's Apache-licensed. It's yours if you want it."
