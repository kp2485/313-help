# CLAUDE.md — handoff for Claude Code

You are building **313 Help** (named by Kyle 2026-09-19), a zero-PII app that points residents of Detroit, Hamtramck, Highland Park and Dearborn to free help, healthy places, parks and transit. (City events are dropped until the City publishes a real feed, and the Events tab hides itself.) **It is live at https://313help.com** (2026-09-21: Pages, the Worker at `/v1`, the steward queue behind Cloudflare Access, nightly publish on) — what merges to `main` reaches residents at the next publish. Read `docs/README.md` first, then `docs/04-resource-lifecycle.md` and `docs/06-architecture.md`. `/README.md` is the public front page and a **showcase** (Kyle, 2026-09-21): about 160 lines, every claim checkable, numbers re-derived from a build; anything still to do appears only as a forward-looking roadmap feature, with no test-environment trivia and no list of internal shortcomings — detail and caveats live in `docs/`. Keep it true when you change what the app does. Every design decision is in `docs/`; if a task conflicts with a doc, stop and ask rather than silently diverging. If you make a decision the docs don't cover, add it to `docs/DECISIONS.md` with a date and one-line rationale.

## Non-negotiables (from docs/01 and docs/08)

- No accounts, no names, no phone numbers, no emails for residents. **No identifier for a resident ever leaves the device.** The only on-device secret is random, resettable, and used solely to derive per-target daily dedupe hashes: `sha256(install_secret ‖ target_id ‖ day)`. If a feature seems to need more, it's out of scope.
- Never log or persist client IPs in the Worker. The Worker source never reads an IP or user-agent header at all (rate limiting is a Cloudflare WAF rule); request bodies are closed schemas with size caps, so an unknown field or an over-long field is a 400 — on the steward routes too, not only the public ones. `api/test` enforces all of this. Timestamps at minute granularity.
- **The Worker logs nothing, and Workers Logs stay off.** `[observability.logs] enabled = false` and `invocation_logs = false` in `api/wrangler.toml`; `onError` and `notFound` log nothing at all (Hono's default `console.error` would have written a D1 error's statement *and its bound values* — a target id, a per-day dedupe hash, a steward's note); the only permitted `console` call is `api/src/log.ts`, whose `FixedMessage` type is an allow-list of two fixed sentences about the nightly cron. Tests enforce both the silence and that no other `console` call exists in `api/src`. Never add request logging, and never paste `wrangler tail` output anywhere.
- **Private kinds are traceless at the list level as well as the detail level.** `isPrivate` in `apps/web/src/needs.ts` is the one definition (`treatment`, `assault`, plus the `shelter.dv` / `health.mental` sensitive pair): those screens write no hash, keep the plain app name as the window title, cannot be saved, and **cannot be shared — `canShare = !isPrivate`**. An arriving private deep link is `replaceState`d out of the URL.
- Triage answers live in memory only and are cleared on exit.
- Nothing is deleted from the dataset; rows are archived with reason.
- Every listing shows freshness **computed on the device** from dated facts in the bundle (never frozen at build time). Badges state facts; never say "verified" for something no person checked. Unknown is never rendered as "open." Reports label rows; they never hide them. A script's match and a person's browser read are **different sentences**: `badge.*.auto_check` says a program matched the page, `badge.*.web` says the website was read and matched. Never collapse them.
- **`entry_method: auto_check` is a claim the build checks.** A row may only say a script matched its page if it has a `source.url` and a `checked_at_entry`, and if that host is not in `data/seed/script-refusing-hosts.csv` on or after the date it started refusing us. Otherwise `pipeline/src/validate.ts` fails the build. Adding a host when a site starts refusing scripts is a steward duty (docs/OPERATIONS.md); a page that answers 200 with almost no text (under 200 visible characters) or with a challenge page counts as **unreadable**, never as a match.
- **On a holiday a schedule-derived "open" becomes the `holiday` status**, carrying the schedule's own times as *usual* hours and never `open`, `closes_at` or `minutes_left`; it ranks exactly where `call_first` ranks. The rule is the eleven US federal holidays plus the observed day, computed **on the device** from a date rule — no list of dates is in the bundle. A steward turns it off for one row with the `flags` value **`open_holidays`**, and only when the owner's own page says the place is open on holidays. Spec: `schema/query-spec.md` "Holidays"; 40 of the 181 fixture cases are `schema/fixtures/12-holidays.json`.
- **Aggregates publish a window, never the cells inside it.** `data/ingested/crashes.json` carries the five-year totals only: per-year numbers were removed because a suppressed "fewer than 5" could be recovered by subtracting the published years from a total (18 cells were recoverable before; 0 are now). Any future indicator that suppresses small counts is held to the same test.
- 911 and 988 are hardcoded and never overridable. Other emergency numbers come from `data/seed/emergency.csv` via the **signed** bundle. We use the number its owner currently publishes: `pnpm check:emergency` reads each number's source page; if the page is read and shows a different number (a mismatch), a release build fails until a person fixes it (DECISIONS 2026-09-19). A page that can't be read is logged for a person, not a failure. The script never rewrites a number; a mismatch is a person's job. Any phone/address/coordinate change from any source is held for steward approval.
- A listing is findable by a phone, a street address, **or** its publisher's own coordinate — one of the three is enough. A coordinate is never reverse-geocoded into an address we then print, and a misprinted phone number is never repaired by guessing the digits.
- **Link-outs hand over the destination and nothing else.** The three buttons that open something outside the app — Directions, Bus directions, and Bus directions in the Transit app (`transit://directions?to=…`, no `from`) — pass the address the place publishes or its coordinate. **Never an origin, never the person's location, never an identifier, never what they were searching for.** Nothing is contacted until the tap: no SDK, no script, no preconnect, no font or icon from anyone's servers; CSP stays `default-src 'self'` and the service worker ignores every origin but ours. Where directions are withheld — DV and mental-health-crisis listings, which carry no address and no coordinate — all three buttons are absent, the Transit one included.
- Bundles are Ed25519-signed; clients pin two public keys (active + spare) and refuse unsigned or mis-signed bundles.
- Harm-reduction, DV, and crisis screens follow the ordering rules in docs/05 (911/hotline first).
- **No address of any kind for a domestic-violence organization, anywhere in this repository** — shelter, office or mailing address; seed data, `internal_note`, research notes, bundle or HSDS export (Kyle, 2026-09-21). A `shelter.dv` row carries a phone and at most one coarse `service_area` from the closed list in `packages/query/src/areas.ts` (a whole city or bigger, never a ZIP or neighbourhood; `statewide` and `national` rank after every local area — the National DV Hotline is `national`). Clients show "Serves {area}" and Call: never a distance, a dot or directions. `pipeline/src/validate.ts` fails the build otherwise (docs/08).

## Build order for the hackathon (docs/09)

All six steps are built; the list stays as the map of the repo. Current test counts are in `/README.md`.

1. `data/seed/` CSVs → `pipeline/` → `data/bundle/v1/` (HSDS-valid + `x_detroit`). Fixture tests for open-now / next-occurrence / ranking in `schema/fixtures/` **before** any UI.
2. `apps/web/` PWA reading the bundle: Home, category list, detail, triage, offline.
3. `api/` Worker + D1: `POST /v1/reports`, `POST /v1/proposals`, steward endpoints behind Cloudflare Access.
4. `admin/` minimal steward queue. Publishing is `.github/workflows/publish.yml` (nightly; `PUBLISH_ENABLED=true` since 2026-09-21) or a local `pnpm build:bundle`.
5. `apps/ios/` SwiftUI app: `DetroitQuery` (the rules), `HelpCore` (privacy-critical logic and the map's pure parts, run in CI), `HelpApp/` (screens, including a full-screen Map tab with both styles).
6. `apps/android/` Kotlin client: platform Views, **no Jetpack Compose, no AndroidX, no dependency of any kind in the APK**, `minSdk` 24. Never describe it as shipped or as tested on a phone until it has been; the PWA installs on Android today. CI builds `:query` and `:core` only and sets **`HELP313_NO_ANDROID=1`** to keep `:app` out on purpose — an ubuntu runner ships a licensed SDK in `ANDROID_HOME` and quietly pulled `:app` in before that flag existed, and a workflow must not accept Google's SDK licence on this repository's behalf.

The four tabs are **Home · Help · Map · Events** (2026-09-20; Recreation and Transit merged into Map). The Map has **two styles**: `standard` (the default on every client) and `subway` (an option). **`docs/MAP-STYLE.md` is the shared spec for all three clients**; the subway data rides in separate, lazily loaded, signed `map/transit/*.net.json` files so the standard files never change by a byte; the text list is identical in both styles, and every map stays operable by keyboard (N/P/Enter) and screen reader.

## Conventions

- TypeScript strict for pipeline/api/web; SwiftUI (iOS 17+) for iOS; Kotlin with platform Android Views (no Compose, no AndroidX, no APK dependencies) for Android. Node 22. pnpm 12 workspaces: dependency install scripts run only when listed under `allowBuilds` in `pnpm-workspace.yaml`, and new package versions must be a day old.
- IDs are stable slugs (`org_`, `loc_`, `svc_`, `sal_`, `alert_`, `rpt_`, plus `plc_` place, `seg_` greenway segment, `cond_` condition report, `prop_` proposal, `nbh_` neighborhood, `ph_` photo key, `emg_` emergency number, `rt_` transit route). HSDS ids are UUIDv5 of the slug; the slug rides in `x_detroit.id`. Never reuse. An ingested layer's `id_prefix` in `data/sources.yaml` (`hr` for the DHD boxes, `wws` for Wayne County's stations) is **not** a new id prefix: it is part of the slug inside `sal_`, as in `sal_wws_dearborn_…`, so this list does not grow when a layer is added.
- Shared query semantics (open-now, next occurrences, badge, ranking) live in `packages/query` with the spec in `schema/query-spec.md` and fixtures in `schema/fixtures/`; web and pipeline import it, iOS re-implements it in `apps/ios/Sources/DetroitQuery` and **Android re-implements it again in `apps/android/query`** (Kotlin, no dependencies). Three implementations, one spec, the same fixtures: a change to the rules is a change in four places, and the fixtures are what keeps them honest.
- Schedules are HSDS/iCal RRULE fields; compute occurrences with a tested library (`rrule` on web/pipeline, used in floating wall-clock mode only — see DECISIONS.md; a small tested Swift implementation or `EventKit`-free custom evaluator on iOS). DST tests are required.
- Detroit time zone `America/Detroit` everywhere. Service area: Detroit, Hamtramck, Highland Park and Dearborn (Kyle, 2026-09-19). Bbox sanity: lat 42.25–42.46, lon −83.33 to −82.91.
- Plain-language UI strings live in `strings/en.json`, with `strings/es.json`, `strings/ar.json` and `strings/bn.json` carrying **the same keys** (tests check keys and placeholders in all four); reading level ≤ 6th grade; no jargon ("Free groceries," not "Food pantry services"). What a place wrote about itself is never machine-translated, in any language. Arabic mirrors the interface right to left; Arabic and Bengali use Western digits so a number reads as it is dialled. **Arabic and Bengali were machine-drafted on 2026-09-20 and no native speaker has read either one** — Spanish has not been reviewed either. Never let a screen, a doc or a store listing imply any of the three was checked by a speaker. The web has a language control in the top bar (a native `<select>`); **the native apps follow the phone's own language list** — the first language we have words for — and have no in-app switch.
- Accessibility: every action has a descriptive label; dynamic type must not truncate phone numbers.
- Commit `data/hsds/` on publish; never commit `data/bundle/`.
- **Privacy-critical native logic lives where CI can run it.** On iPhone that is the `HelpCore` SwiftPM target (`apps/ios/Sources/HelpCore`: the install key and its daily hash, the outbox, the saved-places rules, the one ephemeral session, the signature check, the release rules) — not `HelpApp/`, which needs Xcode. On Android it is the `:core` module, which compiles **the app's own android-free files** (named one by one in `apps/android/core/build.gradle.kts`) on a plain JDK. If you put a rule in a screen, nothing CI runs will ever check it. Two areas still escape: the Swift signature maths and the backup-flag test are inside `#if canImport(CryptoKit)` / `#if canImport(Darwin)`, so on Linux CI they pass while asserting nothing.
- Tests: fixtures for query semantics; schema validation for every bundle build; a Worker test that proves no IP or install_id reaches D1. **Write behavioural tests, not source-text greps.** A test that asserts a source line exists passes when the line is moved, renamed or made dead, and fails on a harmless rewrite. Drive the thing instead: `apps/web/test/behaviour.test.ts` and `apps/web/test/router.test.ts` are the pattern. `apps/web/test/web.test.ts` still holds many source-text assertions; converting them as you touch them is a standing job, not a rewrite to do in one pass.

## Data sources

Registry in `data/sources.yaml` for the layers that become listings (see docs/02 for tiers); the City layers behind the map, parks, ZIPs, events and neighborhood numbers are named at the top of their ingest scripts (`pipeline/src/ingest-*.ts`). Do not scrape any Tier C/D source. No institution maintains a feed for us (DHD included): stewards maintain `data/seed/`; a change in an open-data source arrives as a pull request and is published only when a steward merges it. Page watchers are planned, not built, and would only open steward tasks. All sources are read-only; never write back. Identify our requests honestly; if a site blocks them, check by hand rather than disguising the request.

## Places and condition reports (docs/11, approved)

- Condition reports are about **things, never people**. No category, free-text path, or photo flow for a person, tent, vehicle someone sleeps in, or "suspicious activity." Do not add one, even if asked by a partner; raise it with Kyle.
- Raw GPS never leaves the device; a condition report names the segment whose screen the person opened. Photos are re-drawn on device and stripped of every non-picture block, refused by the server if they carry Exif or any other metadata block, never public, and deleted 30 days after the report closes. Photos are demo-only until the legal advice in docs/11 is in hand.
- Neighborhood indicators (docs/13) use public datasets and our own listings only; never reports, usage, or anything a phone sends. No rankings of neighborhoods, no per-neighborhood crime, small counts suppressed.
